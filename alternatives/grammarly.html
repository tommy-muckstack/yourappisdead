Free Grammarly alternative: GrammarlyIsDead

Last reviewed October 26, 2026 · License: Apache-2.0 · Source on GitHub

GrammarlyIsDead is an open-source writing checker that runs entirely in your browser. It flags spelling, grammar, wordiness, passive voice and formality problems using a dictionary and a set of readable rules, not an AI model. It works only in its own web editor, in US English, and it cannot rewrite sentences for you.

What Grammarly does, and what GrammarlyIsDead covers

Grammarly checks your writing wherever you type: in a browser extension, desktop apps and mobile keyboards. It combines correctness checks with generative AI that can rewrite sentences, adjust tone, and draft text, and its paid plan adds plagiarism and AI-text detection.

GrammarlyIsDead takes a narrower, more transparent approach. You write or paste into its editor, and problems are underlined in four groups. Correctness uses an English (US) Hunspell dictionary for spelling, plus about thirty pattern rules for common mistakes: a/an, its/it's, your/you're, their/there/they're, "could of", then/than, fewer/less, simple subject-verb agreement, repeated words, a lowercase "i", sentence capitals and spacing around punctuation. Clarity flags wordy phrases ("in order to" becomes "to"), stiff word choices, passive voice and sentences over forty words. Engagement catches weak intensifiers and three sentences in a row that start with the same word. Delivery flags slang, all-caps words and piles of exclamation marks, and, if you set a formal goal, contractions.

Each suggestion card offers a fix, alternatives where there are any, Dismiss, and, for spelling, Add to dictionary. An overview panel shows a score, a Flesch readability level, reading and speaking time, and a rough tone label based on keywords. Because the rules are plain code, you can read exactly why something was flagged.

What it costs

As of October 2026, Grammarly's plans page lists a free plan, Pro at US$12 per member per month on annual billing (month-to-month billing costs more), and Enterprise by quote. The free plan covers basic corrections and 100 AI prompts a month, but leaves out tone adjustment, full-sentence rewrites and plagiarism detection.

GrammarlyIsDead has no plans or limits. The hosted copy is free. If you host it yourself, you pay only for your own server and sign-in provider; because checking happens in the visitor's browser, the server does very little work.

How to switch from Grammarly

  1. Open the editor. Each document is saved automatically in your browser, so you can keep several drafts on the dashboard.
  2. Set your goals. Formality matters most: choosing formal turns on contraction and slang checks.
  3. Paste or type your text and work through the cards. Accept, pick an alternative, or dismiss anything you disagree with.
  4. If you built up a personal dictionary in Grammarly, review it in your Grammarly account and add the names and terms you need. Note that capitalized words in the middle of a sentence are skipped by the spell checker anyway.
  5. Copy the finished text back into your email client or document. There is no extension to do this inline.

Running it yourself

The app uses Next.js and TypeScript. Spell checking uses nspell with an SCOWL-derived en_US Hunspell dictionary, whose license file ships in the repository. Run npm install, then npm run dev, and open http://localhost:3000/grammarlyisdead. npm test runs the checking engine's unit tests. Firebase handles sign-in; without its keys the login page says sign-in is not configured, and a demo account is available outside production.

Limitations

  • No generative AI: no rewriting, paraphrasing, shortening or drafting. Rules catch fewer problems than a trained model and will miss errors outside their patterns.
  • Proper nouns are not spell-checked, because capitalized words mid-sentence are skipped.
  • US English only. Audience and domain goals are saved, but only formality meaningfully changes the checks today.
  • Documents are kept in your browser's local storage. There is no sync, so clearing site data removes them.
  • It works only in its own editor. There's no browser extension, desktop app or API yet.

FAQ

Is my writing uploaded anywhere?

No. The dictionary and rules are downloaded to your browser and checks run there. Documents are saved in your browser's local storage.

Will it catch everything Grammarly catches?

No. It handles common, well-defined mistakes well, but it doesn't understand sentence meaning the way an AI model does, so subtler grammar and word-choice problems will slip through.

Can I use it in Gmail or Google Docs?

Not directly. Write or paste in the GrammarlyIsDead editor and copy the result back.

Does it support British English?

Not yet. Only a US English dictionary is included, so British spellings may be flagged.

Can I add my own rules?

Yes, if you run your own copy. Rules are plain TypeScript data in the repository, each with a pattern, a message and a fix.
