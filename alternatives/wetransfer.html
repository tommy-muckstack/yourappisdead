Free WeTransfer alternative: WeTransferIsDead

Last reviewed October 26, 2026 · License: Apache-2.0 · Source on GitHub

WeTransferIsDead is an open-source "upload files, get a link" service with expiring links, optional passwords and a zip download for recipients. No account is needed to send. The important catch: the hosted copy only accepts about 4 MB per transfer because of its hosting platform's request limit, so it's mainly useful for small files or as a starting point for running your own copy.

What WeTransfer does, and what WeTransferIsDead covers

WeTransfer is built for moving large files: you upload, it emails the recipients or gives you a link, and the files expire after a set time. Paid plans raise the transfer and storage limits, remove the monthly transfer cap, and add features such as custom expiry dates and branded download pages.

WeTransferIsDead reproduces the basic loop. You drop in up to 20 files, add an optional message, choose whether the link lasts 1, 3 or 7 days, and optionally set a password. Upload progress is shown, and when it finishes you get a link to copy or an "Email it" button that opens your own mail app with the link filled in. Recipients see your message, each file with its size, and a button to download everything as one zip. They don't need an account either.

As the sender, your browser keeps a private delete key, so you can kill a link before it expires. If you sign in, you also get a history of your transfers with download counts and time remaining.

What it costs

WeTransfer has a free plan and paid Starter, Ultimate and Teams plans, plus Enterprise. As of October 2026, its plan limits page says Free allows 10 transfers and 3 GB in total per rolling 30 days, Starter keeps the 10-transfer limit but raises the total to 300 GB, and Ultimate and Teams remove the transfer count limit. We couldn't load current dollar prices reliably, so check the official pricing page for those.

WeTransferIsDead is free with no plans. Self-hosting is different from the other apps in this series: because files are stored on the server, your costs grow with how much people upload. For anything beyond small files you'll need object storage, which is a real (if usually modest) monthly cost.

How to switch from WeTransfer

There's nothing to migrate, since transfers are temporary on both services. To send your first one:

  1. Open the app. The upload card works without signing in; sign in only if you want a history of transfers.
  2. Add files by dragging them onto the card or clicking to choose. Stay within 20 files and the size limit shown.
  3. Write a message, choose 1, 3 or 7 days, and add a password if the files are private.
  4. Send, then copy the link or use "Email it". If you set a password, give it to the recipient through a different channel.
  5. For anything over a few megabytes, run your own copy with object storage, or keep using a service built for large files.

Running it yourself

It's a Next.js and TypeScript app. Run npm ci, then npm run dev, and open http://localhost:3000/wetransferisdead. Locally, files and metadata are written to disk under ISDEAD_DATA_DIR with a 100 MB limit per transfer. The hosted copy stores file bytes as chunked Firestore documents and caps uploads at 4 MB, because Vercel limits request bodies to 4.5 MB. The README recommends S3, R2 or Google Cloud Storage with signed upload URLs for larger files. Production also needs Firebase keys and an AUTH_SECRET, which signs password unlock cookies.

Limitations

  • The hosted copy's roughly 4 MB limit rules out most of what people use WeTransfer for: video, photo sets, design files.
  • Files aren't encrypted by the app. They're protected by the link, the optional password (stored as a scrypt hash) and expiry.
  • Link IDs are random 12-character strings, but they're generated with JavaScript's non-cryptographic Math.random, so use a password for anything sensitive.
  • Expired transfers are deleted when someone next requests them or a sender's list is loaded, not by a scheduled cleanup.
  • No virus scanning, resumable uploads, previews, branded pages or service-sent emails, and the rate limiter resets whenever the server restarts.

FAQ

Why is the hosted limit so small?

Uploads go through the app's own server, and the hosting platform rejects request bodies over 4.5 MB. Large-file support needs direct-to-storage uploads, which aren't built yet.

Do my recipients need an account?

No. They open the link in any browser and download individual files or a zip.

What happens when a link expires?

The download page shows that the transfer is gone, and the files are deleted the next time the transfer is looked up.

Is the password stored safely?

It's stored as a salted scrypt hash, never in plain text, and is checked on the server before any file is served.

Can I delete a transfer I sent by mistake?

Yes. The browser you sent from holds a delete key, and signed-in users can delete from their transfer list.
