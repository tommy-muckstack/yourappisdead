Free Otter.ai alternative: OtterIsDead

Last reviewed October 26, 2026 · License: Apache-2.0 · Source on GitHub

OtterIsDead is an open-source meeting notes app that transcribes live using your browser's built-in speech recognition, then pulls out an overview, keywords, an outline and action items. It has no minute limits, but it also has no meeting bot, no automatic speaker detection and no AI-written summaries: the insights are rule-based, and transcription quality depends on your browser.

What Otter.ai does, and what OtterIsDead covers

Otter.ai is a hosted AI meeting assistant. It can join Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet calls on its own, transcribe them, identify speakers by name, and produce AI summaries and action items you can share with your team. You can also upload recordings, and ask an AI chat questions about past meetings.

OtterIsDead covers the note-taking side in a single browser. Press record and words appear as people speak, with interim text that firms up as the recognizer settles. You mark who is talking by pressing a number key (1 to 9); you can also type lines, pause and resume, keep notes next to the transcript, and optionally save a copy of the audio on your own device. Afterwards, each conversation has a timestamped transcript you can edit, highlight and search, and renaming a speaker updates every line they said.

The insights panel builds an extractive overview from the transcript's own sentences, along with keywords and an outline split into time chunks. Action items are detected from phrases such as "I'll…", "Priya to…" and "can you…", each with an owner and a due word like "Friday"; you can add, tick off and remove them. An "Ask" box returns the transcript lines most relevant to your question rather than generating an answer.

What it costs

As of October 2026, Otter.ai's pricing page lists a free Basic plan with 300 transcription minutes per user per month and a 30-minute limit per conversation. Pro is listed at US$16.99 per user per month billed monthly, or US$8.33 billed annually, with 1,200 minutes and 90 minutes per conversation. Business is US$30 monthly or US$19.99 annually, with no monthly meeting limit and up to 4 hours per conversation. Enterprise is priced on request. The page also showed temporary discounts at the time we checked, so the price you see may be lower.

OtterIsDead costs nothing to use. Speech recognition is done by your browser rather than a paid API, and conversations are stored in your browser, so a self-hosted copy needs very little server capacity.

How to switch from Otter.ai

  1. In Otter, export each conversation you want to keep as a text or SRT file. Which formats you can export depends on your plan.
  2. Open OtterIsDead and import the file, or paste the transcript. It accepts plain text, .txt, .vtt and .srt.
  3. Rename the speakers once per conversation; every line updates.
  4. If you also have the audio, attach it so clicking a timestamp jumps playback to that moment.
  5. For new meetings, open the app in Chrome, Edge or Safari, allow microphone access and press record. It listens only through your microphone, so in online calls it hears other people only if their voices reach your mic.

Running it yourself

OtterIsDead is a Next.js and TypeScript app. The transcript logic is a set of pure functions in lib/otter/model.ts, covered by unit tests. Run npm install, then npm run dev, and open http://localhost:3000/otterisdead. npm test runs the unit tests and npm run shots runs a Playwright test of the whole flow after a build. Sign-in uses Firebase; without its keys, a demo account is available outside production.

Limitations

  • Speech recognition is provided by your browser vendor, and so are its accuracy, language support and privacy terms. In Chrome, for example, the Web Speech API sends audio to Google's speech service. Firefox isn't supported for live recording; a scripted demo plays instead.
  • No speaker diarization, no offline Whisper model and no meeting bot.
  • Summaries and action items are pattern-based, not written by a language model, so expect misses.
  • Conversations and audio live in your browser (localStorage and IndexedDB). There is no sync or sharing, and clearing site data deletes them; export what you want to keep.

FAQ

Does my audio go to the OtterIsDead server?

No. Recorded audio is kept in your browser. Live transcription, though, is handled by the browser's speech service, which may process audio on the vendor's servers.

Can it tell speakers apart on its own?

No. You press a number key when the speaker changes, then name the speakers afterwards.

Is there a recording time limit?

There's no minute quota. The browser may end a recognition session after silence; the app restarts it automatically.

Can I use it with Zoom or Teams?

Only by recording through your microphone in a browser tab while the call runs. There is no bot or integration that joins calls.

What languages does it support?

It uses your browser's language setting, so support depends on what your browser's speech recognition offers.
