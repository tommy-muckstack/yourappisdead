Free Wispr Flow alternative: WisprFlowIsDead

Last reviewed October 26, 2026 · License: Apache-2.0 · Source on GitHub

WisprFlowIsDead is a free, open-source dictation app for the Mac. You hold a key, speak, and cleaned-up text is typed into whatever app has your cursor. Speech recognition runs on your Mac, and there is no account, word limit or subscription.

What Wispr Flow does, and what WisprFlowIsDead covers

Wispr Flow turns speech into polished writing anywhere you can type. Instead of transcribing word for word, it strips filler words, adds punctuation, follows self-corrections and adapts tone to the app you're in. It also offers a personal dictionary, voice snippets, a command mode for editing selected text, and apps for Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android.

WisprFlowIsDead is a native Swift app that reproduces the desktop dictation workflow on macOS. Hold fn (or ⌃⌥ on keyboards without one) to talk, or press fn + space for hands-free mode. A small floating "Flow Bar" shows your audio level while you speak. When you release the key, the text is cleaned up and pasted at the cursor, and your clipboard is restored afterwards.

Transcription uses NVIDIA's Parakeet model through the FluidAudio library, which downloads about 600 MB once and then runs locally. On macOS 26 you can use Apple's built-in recognizer instead. The stronger cleanup levels and the experimental Command Mode use Apple's on-device language model, so they need macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence available; on older systems a rule-based cleanup is used.

What it costs

As of October 2026, Wispr Flow's pricing page lists a free plan capped at 2,000 words a week on desktop, and Pro at $15 per user per month, or $12 when billed annually, with no word limit. Growth and Enterprise tiers add team features and meeting notes on top.

WisprFlowIsDead is free with no usage cap. Because recognition and cleanup run on your own hardware, there's no per-word cost and no API key to buy.

How to switch from Wispr Flow

  1. Check your Mac: the current release (0.1.1) is built for Apple silicon and needs macOS 14.2 or later.
  2. Download the DMG from the project page or the GitHub releases page, open it and drag the app into Applications. The 0.1.1 build is signed and notarized, so it opens without Gatekeeper workarounds.
  3. Quit Wispr Flow first, or change one app's shortcut, so both aren't listening for fn.
  4. Grant Microphone access when asked, then Accessibility in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Without Accessibility the app can't type into other apps and puts the text on your clipboard instead.
  5. Let the speech model download on first run, then re-enter the names and jargon from your Wispr Flow dictionary and any snippets you rely on. There is no importer.

Running it yourself

The app is a Swift package. Sources/FlowCore holds the pure logic (shortcut state machine, styles, cleanup, dictionary, snippets, history), Sources/FlowVoice handles the microphone, speech-to-text and text insertion, and Sources/WisprFlowIsDead is the SwiftUI and AppKit interface. Run scripts/run-dev.sh to build, wrap and launch a development copy, and swift test for the core tests. Updates are delivered through Sparkle with signed update feeds, and you can trigger a check from the menu bar.

Limitations

  • Mac only, and the published installer targets Apple silicon. Intel Macs and other platforms aren't covered.
  • The best cleanup levels and Command Mode require macOS 26; older systems get the simpler rule-based cleanup.
  • The first run needs a roughly 600 MB model download before dictation works.
  • The project's own status notes say live dictation into real apps, and typing while macOS Secure Input is active, haven't been fully verified by hand yet. Expect some rough edges.
  • No team features, sync between devices, or mobile keyboard.

FAQ

Does my voice leave my Mac?

Not for dictation. Transcription runs locally through Parakeet or Apple's recognizer, and cleanup uses either rules or Apple's on-device model. The app doesn't need an account or an AI key.

Does it work offline?

After the one-time model download, the dictation pipeline runs on the device, so it doesn't depend on a connection.

Can I change the hotkey?

Yes. Every shortcut can be rebound, with up to four bindings per action, including extra mouse buttons.

What happens if I say the wrong thing?

Say "scratch that" or "no wait" mid-sentence, or use "Undo AI edit" in History to get back the raw transcript.

Will it work on an Intel Mac?

The released installer is described as Apple silicon only. You could try building from source, but that isn't a documented, supported path.
