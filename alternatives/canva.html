Free Canva alternative: CanvaIsDead

Last reviewed October 26, 2026 · License: Apache-2.0 · Source on GitHub

CanvaIsDead is an open-source, browser-based design editor for social posts, slides, posters and similar one-off graphics. It covers the core layout work (shapes, text, images, pages, export) without watermarks or plan gates, but it has no stock media library, no collaboration and no AI tools, and your designs are stored only in the browser you made them in.

What Canva does, and what CanvaIsDead covers

Canva is a hosted design platform. Beyond its drag-and-drop editor, much of its value is in the content around it: a very large template and stock library, brand kits, team folders, real-time collaboration, video editing, and a growing set of AI features. If you rely on those, CanvaIsDead will not replace them.

What CanvaIsDead does cover is the editor itself. You pick a preset size or enter custom dimensions (pixels, inches, millimeters or centimeters), then build the design from shapes, text and your own uploaded images. Elements can be dragged, resized and rotated with snapping guides, grouped (including nested groups), locked, aligned, layered and nudged with the keyboard. Text supports a handful of fonts, lists, letter and line spacing, effects such as shadow, outline and neon, and curved text. Images can be cropped and flipped. Designs can have multiple pages, and a presentation mode includes speaker notes, a separate presenter window and autoplay.

Downloads are rendered locally in your browser as PNG, JPG, SVG or PDF, for all pages or a chosen range, with no watermark.

What it costs

As of October 2026, Canva's pricing page lists a free plan, Canva Pro at US$144 a year for one person, and Canva Business at US$250 a year per person, with Enterprise priced on request. A monthly billing option is also offered. The free plan is genuinely usable; the paid plans mainly add premium content, brand tools, background removal and more AI usage.

CanvaIsDead has no paid tier. The hosted copy is free to use, and if you run it yourself the only cost is whatever your hosting and sign-in provider charge. Because designs are stored in your browser rather than on a server, there are no storage fees to plan for.

How to switch from Canva

There is no importer for Canva design files, so switching means rebuilding rather than migrating. A practical approach:

  1. In Canva, download the finished designs you want to keep as PNG or PDF so you have a reference copy.
  2. Download any photos and logos you uploaded to Canva, as originals if you still have them. CanvaIsDead accepts PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF and AVIF uploads.
  3. Open CanvaIsDead, choose Create, and pick a matching size or enter the exact dimensions of the original design.
  4. Add your images through Uploads, then rebuild the layout with text, shapes and elements. Use Position for exact sizes and coordinates.
  5. Download the result, then use Back up designs on the home screen and keep that file somewhere safe, since the browser is the only other copy.

Running it yourself

CanvaIsDead is a Next.js 15 and React 19 app written in TypeScript. Sign-in uses Firebase (email and password, or Google), and PDF output uses pdf-lib. It needs Node.js 24.12 or newer. Clone the repository, run npm install and then npm run dev; the app opens at http://localhost:3000/canvaisdead. Without Firebase environment variables the login page reports that sign-in is not configured, but a demo account is available outside production. The repository also includes unit tests (npm test), linting, type checks and a Playwright browser suite.

Limitations

  • Designs and uploads live in your browser's IndexedDB storage. There is no cloud sync, so clearing site data deletes them unless you have a backup file.
  • No stock library, brand kits, collaboration, comments, video, animation or AI features.
  • PDFs are flattened images: text in them is not selectable, and there is no CMYK, bleed or crop-mark support for professional printing.
  • Photo filters, crop straightening and background removal are unfinished, and the font selection is small.
  • The project describes itself as a work in progress. Its README notes that many browser tests were failing on the first automated Linux run and that visual behavior still needs verification.

FAQ

Can I open my existing Canva designs?

No. There is no Canva file import. You can reuse exported images and your original uploads, but layouts have to be rebuilt.

Do downloads have a watermark?

No. Every export format is available without a watermark or a paid plan.

Where are my designs stored?

In IndexedDB in the browser you are using, scoped to your account. They are not uploaded to a server, which also means they don't follow you to another device.

Is it good enough for print work?

For home printing or simple flyers, the 300 DPI PDF option may be enough. For a commercial printer that needs CMYK, bleed or vector text, it is not suitable yet.

Can my team edit the same design together?

Not yet. There is no sharing or real-time collaboration; each person works in their own browser.
